Woah, thanks for sharing the charts, Alma.  We now have the benefit of answering your question AFTER the post, but here's here my take:

Customer TailPut Vomma (how vega changes when vol changes)
Customers are long tail puts. Their vega sensitivity to vol increases is massive early in the week (4-8M at 3/14 with +60% IV) and decays sharply toward 3/20. Vomma shrinks as time to expiry shrinks.  If vol spikes Monday, these tail puts gain enormous vega exposure (their vol sensitivity compounds on itself). By Friday, the same vol spike produces a fraction of the vega gain. The customer's purchased convexity is a wasting asset…. most potent now, weakest at

Customer ATM Vomma
Customers vomma is entirely negative, reaching -8M to -10M in the late-week/high-vol corner. When vol rises, customers' short ATM position bleeds accelerating vega.  They become more short vega exactly as vol is expanding, and this gets worse as the week progresses. Assuming this is an iron fly's it highlights a core risk: the body punishes more severely as time passes and vol rises. Early in the week, the wings (shown in the first image) offset this pain. By Friday, the wings have decayed and the body dominates.

Customer TailPut Zomma (how gamma changes when vol changes)
Customers' tail puts gain gamma when vol rises (+2,500-3,000 at 3/14 with +60% IV), front-loaded and decaying like vomma but at a slower decay rate. This is like a bridge between vol space and spot space: a vol spike makes customers' puts more gamma-rich, meaning their delta accelerates faster toward the tails. Early-week vol spikes give customers responsive, convex protection. By Friday, the same vol spike gives them much less additional gamma — their tail puts behave more like static delta positions and less like convex options.

Customer ATM Zomma
Customers are short the ATM presumably, so early in the week if vol drops, their short gamma exposure intensifies. Late in the week if vol rises, their short gamma exposure deepens dramatically. The crossover from positive to negative occurs approximately mid-week.  Before Wednesday, the ATM zomma environment is still partially constructive for an iron fly. After Wednesday, it turns destructive — the body's gamma risk accelerates in a vol-up scenario, and the wings (from TailPut Zomma chart) have decayed too much to compensate.
